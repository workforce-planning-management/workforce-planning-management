# Injury reporting and separation duties, by jurisdiction

The research of WPM-T412, on 2026-11-05, for the eleven jurisdictions of [statutory-pay-protections.md](statutory-pay-protections.md): what an employer must report when someone is hurt at work, what it must hand over when someone leaves, and what that means for the injury record (WPM-R221, WPM-D179).

> ⚠️ **Not legal advice, and not reviewed by a lawyer.** An AI assistant did this research. The aim was the primary source in its original language, which often was not reachable, so each row says what was read. Every figure is a dated parameter that a person confirms before anyone relies on it (WPM-T411, WPM-D155). Nothing here goes into a jurisdiction profile on the strength of this page.

## How to read this

- **Primary** is the statute, regulation or gazette on an official host. **Mirror** is that text on a private host, used when the official host could not be reached. **Guidance** is the authority's own page or form. **Secondary** is anything else.
- **Statute days** means the source gives a number of days and this reading did not settle whether they are calendar or working days. A person must settle it before using the figure. Sources that say so are marked as calendar or working days.
- **Not found** means it was looked for and not confirmed. Nothing was filled from memory. A negative ("no statutory premium") is weaker than a positive.
- Several pages were read through a small extraction model, with short quotes, so quoted figures need a human check.

Access limits: `law.go.kr` and `gesetze-im-internet.de` could not be reached, so **all Korean and German statute text here comes from mirrors** (nepla.ai; sozialgesetzbuch-sgb.de and buzer.de). `legifrance.gouv.fr` and `ameli.fr` refused the request, so French code text is from codes.droit.org (edition of 2026-11-04) and `code.travail.gouv.fr`. `moel.go.kr` pages are excerpts only. Indian gazettes were read on third-party hosts, not `egazette.gov.in`. Rulings and statutes on these hosts should be read again on the official site.

## The finding that matters

**A report and a record are different things, and in every jurisdiction read the statutory record holds what WPM-D158 refuses to hold.** OSHA's Form 301, RIDDOR's record, Germany's Unfallanzeige, France's minor accidents register, Denmark's report, Japan's report, Korea's kept record and India's Form XI all require a description of what happened, and most name the injury or the body part. So WPM's row can prove that a report was made, when, to whom and at what class. It can never be the statutory record, and it must point to where that record is held. See [what this means for WPM](#what-this-means-for-wpm).

## When someone leaves

The word "separation record" hides very different things. **The first pass said a US employer owes a notice to the state agency. All three state duties found are notices to the employee.** In several countries a document exists only on request, and in Japan a certificate must not say more than was asked.

| Jurisdiction | What is owed | When | Must it state the reason? | Basis, as read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

**What this does to WPM-T422:** the separation record is a reasonable common core, and the country forms stay outside WPM. A German Arbeitsbescheinigung and a Spanish certificate state the reason, so whoever issues them needs it, which fits HR holding it (WPM-D178). A generator for Japan's certificate would have to leave out anything the worker did not ask for. WPM has no generator.

## Other gaps closed

- **United States minimum wage:** $7.25 federal since 24 July 2009. The DOL state table, marked 1 July 2026, is out of date for Florida, which rose to $15.00 on 30 September 2026 (secondary), so it cannot be a live source for a dated floor.
- **Germany, overtime:** no statutory premium, on a chamber of commerce's plain statement. Case law not read.
- **Japan, overtime:** the 50 percent band reached small employers on **1 April 2023**. The guide that said 2025 was wrong.
- **South Korea, overtime:** under current law **holiday hours count** towards the 52-hour week, from 1 July 2018. The "disagreement" was old law against new. The Supreme Court's 2023 decision, 2020도15393, is about how the 12 hours are measured, not this. Its text came from an unverified third-party copy.
- **India, overtime:** the Code on Wages, section 14 included, is in force from 21 November 2025. The national floor wage was still not notified on 12 May 2026.
- **UK holiday pay:** regular overtime counts in a week's pay for the first 4 weeks of leave (reg 16(3ZA), from 1 January 2024). Reg 16A is rolled-up holiday pay, not this. Six-year records under reg 16B are reported by a government consultation; the regulation itself was not read.
- **UK minimum wage:** accommodation offset £11.10 a day and £77.70 a week; apprentice rate £8.00 an hour, from April 2026.

## What this means for WPM

Requirement WPM-R221 as first written cannot do the job. It held a date, a severity class and one reported flag.

- **One incident owes several reports.** China has three duties, Sweden and Germany two recipients, India a report and a notice to the family. So reports are their own rows.
- **A date is too coarse.** The US, China and India count in hours, so the record needs a time, and the time the employer learned of it.
- **"Lost time or not" expresses no country's rule.** Triggers are counts of days, or of hours in India, or whether a benefit may follow in Sweden. WPM holds a plain class and the number of days, and leaves the rule to the person filing.
- **A deadline may be "immediately".** Sweden gives no number, so a due time is optional.
- **Not every incident has a worker.** A dangerous occurrence has no injured person, and a non-worker has no record in WPM.
- **An incident can touch several people.** Spain, Korea and China count them.
- **Retention is often unknown.** It was not found for the report itself in Germany, Spain, France, Sweden, Denmark, Japan or China, so a keep-until date is optional, never guessed.

Not built, and each needs a decision: a country's own severity words; the US privacy case list, whose flag would itself say something sensitive; the safety representative's and the worker's copies; Korea's confirmation by the worker representative; and China's decision date.

## Not found

- The retention period of the report itself, in seven of the eleven; whether each portal keeps a copy for the employer.
- US: Texas Labor Code 409.005 and Cal. Labor Code 6409.1 text; an employer's duty to keep a copy of the 8-hour or 24-hour report; separation notices in Texas, Florida and Illinois; a federal statement that no general notice exists.
- UK: the funding source of IIDB, stated outright; the text of reg 16B.
- Germany: an immediate duty in §193 itself. Spain: whether the certificate text of 10 statute days was repealed. France: the form's detail and the keeping of the declaration. Sweden: whether a certificate is still demanded for new cases. Denmark: the employer percentage under ALS §83, any 2026 amounts.
- Japan: the day count, confirmed in the statute. Korea: the 0.56 to 18.56 range; the 2026 commuting rate from MOEL. China: any 2026 national unemployment rate; the original 人社部发〔2015〕71号. India: whether section 164(2)(b) commenced; state rules; replacement ESI regulations; a successor to the section 10B duty of seven statute days; an EPF or ESIC exit filing.
